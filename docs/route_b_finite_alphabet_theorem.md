# Route B finite-alphabet event-separation theorem

Status: **candidate theorem and proof audit; not a novelty claim**  
Date: 2026-10-20

## Purpose and scope

This note asks whether a simple finite model can derive positive event-level
separation from source redundancy, sparse manipulation, persistence and a
minimum mechanism effect. It does not solve unknown-boundary online detection
and does not authorize a D-MIR implementation.

The model is deliberately binary. This is already a finite-alphabet instance
of the proposed problem and makes every assumption and counterexample explicit.

## Model

There are \(S\) sources and a finite situation set \(\mathcal Z\). Each
situation has a clean binary source signature

\[
c_z\in\{0,1\}^S,
\qquad
d=\min_{z\ne z'}d_H(c_z,c_{z'}).
\]

The audit conditions on a candidate change boundary, a pre-boundary situation
\(z_0\), and a pre-boundary mechanism class. These quantities must have been
identified from earlier observations; the true post-boundary event remains
hidden.

An attack path over a window of length \(L\) is
\(a=(a_1,\ldots,a_L)\), where \(a_t\in\{0,1\}^S\). At every time,

\[
\lVert a_t\rVert_0\le k.
\]

Let \(\mathcal A_0\) be all admissible paths for the continuing mechanism and
\(\mathcal A_1\) all admissible paths for a changed mechanism. The exact path
and affected sources are unknown. The structural minimum mechanism effect is

\[
r=\min_{a\in\mathcal A_0,\,b\in\mathcal A_1}d_H(a,b)>0,
\]

where block Hamming distance sums over source-time coordinates. Thus a mechanism
change cannot select a manipulation path also allowed by the continuing
mechanism. This is a restriction on mechanism actions, not an assumption that
the four event observation distributions are already separated.

A situation change persists throughout the window. Before measurement noise,
the report block generated by situation \(z\) and path \(a\) is

\[
y(z,a)=
(c_z\oplus a_1,\ldots,c_z\oplus a_L)
\in\{0,1\}^{SL}.
\]

The four event-center sets are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal C_0 &= \{y(z_0,a):a\in\mathcal A_0\},\\
\mathcal C_Z &= \{y(z,a):z\ne z_0,\ a\in\mathcal A_0\},\\
\mathcal C_M &= \{y(z_0,a):a\in\mathcal A_1\},\\
\mathcal C_{ZM} &= \{y(z,a):z\ne z_0,\ a\in\mathcal A_1\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The attacker and transition model may randomize over any center within the
appropriate set. They must choose non-anticipatively: they cannot observe future
measurement-noise bits before choosing the attack path.

Finally, each of the \(n=SL\) bits passes independently through a binary
symmetric channel with crossover probability \(0\le\eta<1/2\). Write
\(K_\eta\) for this block channel. For event \(h\),
\(\mathcal P_h^{(L)}\) contains \(K_\eta\mu\) for every probability
distribution \(\mu\) supported on \(\mathcal C_h\).

## Lemma 1: event-center separation

Assume \(d>2k\) and \(r>0\). For any two distinct event classes, every pair of
their centers has Hamming distance at least

\[
D=\min\{r,\,L(d-2k)\}>0.
\]

### Proof

If two events have the same post-boundary situation but different mechanism
classes, XOR by the common repeated signature cancels. Their center distance is
the distance between a path in \(\mathcal A_0\) and one in
\(\mathcal A_1\), which is at least \(r\).

If their post-boundary situations differ, consider one time step. For two
attack masks \(a_t,b_t\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
d_H(c_z\oplus a_t,c_{z'}\oplus b_t)
&\ge d_H(c_z,c_{z'})
-d_H(a_t,0)-d_H(b_t,0)\\
&\ge d-2k.
\end{aligned}
\]

The situation persists for \(L\) steps, so summing over the block gives at
least \(L(d-2k)\). Pairs such as \(H_Z\) versus \(H_{ZM}\) may use the same
post-change situation, giving the first case, or different situations, giving
the second. Taking the minimum covers all six event pairs. \(\square\)

This lemma implies that the four noiseless center sets are disjoint. More
strongly, their radius-\(\lfloor(D-1)/2\rfloor\) Hamming neighborhoods are
disjoint.

## Theorem 1: uniform finite-window separation

Under the model above, assume \(d>2k\), \(r>0\), and \(0\le\eta<1/2\).
Then

\[
\delta_L=
\min_{h\ne h'}
\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_h^{(L)},\,Q\in\mathcal P_{h'}^{(L)}}
\operatorname{TV}(P,Q)>0.
\]

Two explicit lower bounds are available. First,

\[
\boxed{\delta_L\ge(1-2\eta)^{SL}.}
\]

Second, let

\[
\alpha_D=
\Pr\!\left\{B\ge\left\lceil D/2\right\rceil\right\},
\qquad B\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(SL,\eta).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\delta_L\ge\max\{(1-2\eta)^{SL},\,1-2\alpha_D\}.}
\]

The first bound proves strict positivity for every \(\eta<1/2\). The second is
often more informative when the event-center distance is large relative to the
expected number of noise flips.

### Proof

Before measurement noise, any two event distributions \(\mu,\nu\) have
disjoint support by Lemma 1. Hence
\(\operatorname{TV}(\mu,\nu)=1\), or
\(\lVert\mu-\nu\rVert_1=2\).

The one-bit binary symmetric channel is invertible for \(\eta<1/2\), and the
induced \(\ell_1\) operator norm of its inverse is
\((1-2\eta)^{-1}\). For \(SL\) independent bits,

\[
\lVert K_\eta^{-1}\rVert_{1\to1}=(1-2\eta)^{-SL}.
\]

Therefore

\[
2=\lVert\mu-\nu\rVert_1
\le(1-2\eta)^{-SL}
\lVert K_\eta\mu-K_\eta\nu\rVert_1,
\]

which yields
\(\operatorname{TV}(K_\eta\mu,K_\eta\nu)
\ge(1-2\eta)^{SL}\). The inequality is uniform over all mixtures within the
two event-center sets.

For the distance-sensitive bound, decode an observed block to the event whose
center set has minimum Hamming distance. If fewer than \(D/2\) bits are flipped,
the received block remains closer to its true event-center set than to every
other event-center set. Thus the event error probability is at most
\(\alpha_D\), uniformly over centers and mixtures.

For any two events \(h,h'\), let \(A_h\) be the set on which this decoder outputs
\(h\). Under any \(P\in\mathcal P_h^{(L)}\),
\(P(A_h)\ge1-\alpha_D\); under any
\(Q\in\mathcal P_{h'}^{(L)}\), \(Q(A_h)\le\alpha_D\). Therefore

\[
\operatorname{TV}(P,Q)\ge P(A_h)-Q(A_h)\ge1-2\alpha_D.
\]

Combining the two bounds proves the theorem. \(\square\)

## Corollary: a computable sufficient window condition

The distance-sensitive bound is positive whenever \(\alpha_D<1/2\). A simpler
conservative condition follows from Hoeffding's inequality. If

\[
\frac{D}{2SL}>
\eta+\sqrt{\frac{\log 2}{2SL}},
\]

then \(\alpha_D<1/2\) and the nearest-event decoder supplies a nonzero explicit
separation certificate.

This condition is sufficient, not necessary. The inverse-channel bound already
proves mathematical positivity for every \(\eta<1/2\), but it can be
exponentially small and operationally useless. A publishable result needs a
separation rate that remains meaningful in the intended source and window
regime.

## Assumption-deletion counterexamples

### 1. Remove source redundancy: \(d\le2k\)

Choose two clean signatures whose difference set has at most \(2k\) positions.
Partition that set into \(U,V\) with \(|U|,|V|\le k\). Let one attack flip
\(U\) and the other flip \(V\). Then

\[
c_z\oplus 1_U=c_{z'}\oplus1_V.
\]

A situation change under one admissible attack is observationally identical to
no situation change or a different event under the other. State recovery and
event attribution both fail.

### 2. Remove the minimum mechanism effect: \(r=0\)

If \(\mathcal A_0\cap\mathcal A_1\ne\varnothing\), choose the same attack path
from the intersection. Then \(H_0\) and \(H_M\) have an identical center;
likewise \(H_Z\) and \(H_{ZM}\) can coincide. Sparse manipulation alone cannot
make a mechanism transition observable.

### 3. Remove persistence or path constraints

Let a one-step situation change mix equally between
\(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(0.2)\) and
\(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(0.8)\), while a mechanism change produces
\(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(0.5)\). The event laws are equal at that time. If all
successor states immediately merge into a common state, they remain equal for
every later observation window. A duration lower bound excludes this example
only when combined with a block-level emission or path-separation condition.

### 4. Allow a completely randomizing observation channel

At \(\eta=1/2\), every center induces the uniform distribution on observed
blocks. All four event-law sets coincide and \(\delta_L=0\), regardless of
source redundancy or mechanism effect.

### 5. Remove identifiable pre-boundary context

If the pre-boundary situation or mechanism class is unrestricted, a post-block
generated by continuing in \((z_1,m_1)\) can equal one generated by changing
from \((z_0,m_0)\) into \((z_1,m_1)\). Post-boundary reports alone identify at
most the new composite state. Event attribution requires a sufficiently
identified pre-boundary state/class or a joint theorem that includes the
pre-boundary window.

### 6. Allow the attacker to anticipate measurement noise

An attacker that sees future channel flips can choose masks that cancel or mimic
them, invalidating both the binomial error calculation and the fixed-channel
inverse argument. The theorem requires non-anticipating manipulation.

## What this theorem does and does not contribute

The proof combines classical coding-distance logic, finite-channel
invertibility and a nearest-set decoder. Those ingredients are established and
must not be presented as new. The useful result of this audit is narrower:

- it gives one internally consistent set of assumptions under which all four
  event-law sets remain separated even after arbitrary randomization over
  admissible sparse attack paths;
- it identifies exactly why state recovery alone does not detect a mechanism
  change;
- it exposes the strong assumptions still hidden by the simplified theorem:
  a known candidate boundary, identifiable prehistory, persistent situation,
  binary XOR manipulation, separated mechanism-action classes and independent
  noise.

The next theoretical question is whether event separation can be retained when
the boundary and pre-state are uncertain and when mechanism effects are defined
through observable cross-source constraints rather than separated latent attack
sets. Until that question and the direct-prior-art audit are resolved, this
theorem does not pass the Route B algorithm-development gate.
