# Route B pre-implementation GO/NO-GO protocol

Status: **active gate; implementation is not authorized by this document**

## Purpose

This protocol prevents a new algorithm from being built before the research
distinction is defensible. Passing a gate permits the next stage; it does not
establish novelty or empirical success.

## Gate 1: direct-prior-art audit

**GO** only after the full-text search in `route_b_literature_scope.md` is
completed and a comparison table covers at least BOCPD, switching/factorial
state-space models, secure state estimation, sensor-fault isolation,
adversarial HMM inference and reject-option prediction.

For each closest method, the audit must answer whether it jointly represents
physical state and observation mechanism, attributes an online change between
them, gives identification assumptions, and abstains under ambiguity.

**NO-GO or redefine the claim** if a reproducible method already provides the
same outputs under equal or weaker assumptions and the proposed work adds no
new theorem, decision rule or adversarial setting.

Current status: **PENDING** because scholarly full text was unavailable through
the managed cloud proxy.

## Gate 2: mathematical distinction

Before code is written, produce all of the following:

1. one explicit pair of situation-change and mechanism-change models whose
   observation block laws are identical;
2. one restricted multi-source model in which the two changes are identifiable;
3. a proof or cited theorem for the restricted case;
4. a computable diagnostic linked to the proof assumptions;
5. a statement of what `insufficient evidence` means under model mismatch.

**GO** if the restricted case is operationally meaningful and the diagnostic is
not merely posterior entropy under a renamed composite HMM.

**NO-GO** if identification requires direct access to the true mechanism label,
the hidden situation, or an oracle channel unavailable at inference time.

Current status: **PARTIAL, WITH A CORRECTED CLAIM**. The audit found that
state-level emission injectivity does not imply event-level identifiability when
events mix over successor paths. `route_b_identifiability_audit.md` now contains
a finite-state counterexample, the correct event-law/convex-hull criterion, and
an anchor-free sparse-source direction. A binary finite-alphabet candidate
theorem now derives a positive bound under a known candidate boundary,
identifiable prehistory, persistent state, separated sparse mechanism-action
classes and independent bit noise. Gate 2 remains open because these assumptions
are strong, the theorem's ingredients are classical, and its relationship to
fault-isolation and secure-estimation results is not yet resolved.

## Gate 3: simulator validity

Freeze a minimal simulator before algorithm tuning. It must contain the four
cases in `route_b_problem_formulation.md`, preserve the overall report budget
across paired cases, and record the realized block separation or a justified
proxy. The ambiguity case must be generated by construction rather than by
adding arbitrary noise.

The simulator must also expose a setting where a true situation transition and
a strategic emission change cause similar short-term reports but diverge with
additional sources or time. Otherwise there is no meaningful delay-versus-
identifiability problem to study.

**NO-GO** if the event type can be recovered from a leaked feature, scenario
name, fixed timestamp, missingness artifact or mechanism label.

Current status: **NOT STARTED**.

## Gate 4: minimal algorithm comparison

Only after Gates 1–3 pass, implement the smallest finite-state model. Required
baselines are:

- archived fixed RCL;
- Huber robust likelihood;
- fixed-mechanism Bayesian filter;
- standard BOCPD;
- matched switching or factorial HMM;
- entropy/confidence abstention at matched coverage.

All methods receive the same online fields and training-label budget. An oracle
mechanism-label model may be reported only as an upper bound.

The development result is a **GO to formal evaluation** only if the candidate:

1. improves event-attribution log loss or error over the matched switching model;
2. reduces false situation flips after mechanism-only changes;
3. does not materially delay detection of genuine situation changes at the
   frozen false-alarm rate; and
4. has lower selective risk in constructed ambiguous cases at matched coverage.

Numerical margins, seeds and confidence intervals must be frozen after a small
wiring/calibration pilot and before the development comparison. A gain against
RCL alone is insufficient.

**NO-GO** if gains disappear when supervision is matched, if the standard
switching model matches them, or if the method remains confident in the
constructed non-identifiable case.

Current status: **BLOCKED BY GATES 1–3**.

## Gate 5: formal claim boundary

Formal evaluation must use fresh seeds and unchanged paired scenarios. The
independent statistical unit is the training or simulator seed, not each time
step. Detection delay must be compared at a fixed false-alarm operating point;
selective prediction must be compared at matched coverage.

Even after a positive result, the allowed claim is limited to the stated model
class and identification assumptions. The method may be described as
model-based attribution, not guaranteed recognition of deception in every
environment.

## Immediate decision

Do not implement D-MIR yet. Complete the fault-isolation and secure-estimation
parts of Gate 1, then prove a useful finite-window event-separation result for
Gate 2 with uncertain boundary/prehistory or an equally operational extension.
The current finite-alphabet theorem is a feasibility result, not sufficient
authorization for implementation. State-identification results alone do not
pass the gate. RCL remains a frozen baseline and evidence source; protocol v1
must not be rerun or modified.
